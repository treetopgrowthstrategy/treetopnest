# Extraction prompts: pulling the content out of Bill

You do not draft. You dump, I shape.

**The loop, per piece:**
1. You open a Claude session and paste one prompt block below.
2. You answer however is fastest. Voice-to-text, half sentences, pasted Slack, old deck excerpts, call notes. Grammar does not matter. Skip any question you have nothing real for.
3. I turn it into the finished page, written against the target keyword cluster.
4. You do one pass and flag anything that does not sound like you.

**Budget:** about 45 minutes dumping, 30 minutes reviewing. Two pieces a month fits inside 8 to 12 hours.

**The one rule that makes this work:** every answer should contain something a competitor could not write. A number, a name, a date, a fight you had, a thing you got wrong. If an answer would be true for any fractional CMO, it is not usable and I will come back and ask again.

## Why these four pages, and why this angle

Live Ahrefs and SERP data, 2026-07-31:

- `fractional cmo` is **7,900 volume at difficulty 3**, CPC $600, traffic potential 6,200.
- A **DR-2 site with 6 referring domains** currently ranks page one for `fractional cmo cost`. Authority is not the gate on this SERP.
- Page one for `fractional cmo` is dominated by **Reddit and LinkedIn skepticism**. Real titles ranking today: "Fractional CMO is a stupid term", "80% of fractional CMOs on LinkedIn are freelance consultants who renamed themselves", "sometimes you shouldn't hire a fractional CMO."

Google is serving buyers honest insider criticism of this category, not service pages. You are a practicing fractional CMO with nine named engagements. You can write that criticism with standing that nobody else on that SERP has.

**So the voice for all four pages is: the practitioner telling the truth about his own category, including the parts that cost him money.** Not a pitch. The pitch is implied by the honesty.

## Prompt 1: `/fractional-cmo` (the hub)

*Targets: `fractional cmo` (7,900), `what does a fractional cmo do` (250), `why hire a fractional cmo` (traffic potential 6,100). This page has to beat Reddit at honesty.*

```
I'm writing the main fractional CMO page for my site. The Google results for
"fractional cmo" are dominated by Reddit and LinkedIn posts calling the category
a scam or a rebranded freelancer. I want to write the honest insider version.
Interview me. Here's my dump:

1. When someone asks what you actually do all day on a fractional engagement,
   what's the real answer? Walk me through a specific week from a specific
   client, not a generic one.

2. The Reddit critique is that 80% of "fractional CMOs" are freelance
   consultants who renamed themselves. Is that true? What percentage would you
   guess, and what specifically separates the real ones?

3. What's the single most common thing a client expected from you that a
   fractional CMO genuinely cannot deliver? Give me the conversation.

4. Name a time you told a prospect not to hire you, or not to hire a fractional
   CMO at all. What was the situation and what did you tell them?

5. Across Factor_, Hapana, EZO.io, Synchrony, Gold's Gym, Stack System, Axle,
   Eat Clean, and the connected fitness portfolio: which engagement went best
   and why, and which was hardest and why? Be specific about what made the
   difference.

6. What does the first 30 days actually look like? What do you do in week one
   that a full-time hire wouldn't, and what do you deliberately not touch?

7. What's the failure mode you see most often in engagements that go wrong?
   Is it the client, the fractional, or the setup?

8. There's a 94-slide-deck critique going around: fractionals who produce
   strategy documents and no execution. How do you avoid being that? What do
   you actually ship?

9. What do you refuse to do in an engagement, and why?

10. If you could tell every founder considering this one thing, what is it?
```

## Prompt 2: `/fractional-cmo-cost`

*Targets: `fractional cmo cost` (250, difficulty 1), `fractional cmo salary` (250), `fractional cmo hourly rate` (150), `fractional cmo pricing` (100), `how much does a fractional cmo cost` (100). Combined CPC is high and difficulty is near zero. Anchor this on the restored 2026 pricing report so it cites real data instead of invented ranges.*

```
I'm writing the pricing page for fractional CMO work. Every competing page
either cites nothing or cites the other pages. I want mine to be the one with
real numbers and a stated methodology. Interview me. Here's my dump:

1. What do you actually charge right now? Give me the real structure: retainer
   bands, hours, minimum term, what's in and what's out.

2. Walk me through the last three times you quoted a number and the client
   pushed back. What did they say, what did you do, where did it land?

3. What's the cheapest engagement you'd take, and what's the number below which
   you say no? Why is that the line?

4. Where do the published numbers on other sites get it wrong? Too high, too
   low, or the wrong shape entirely?

5. What do clients consistently misunderstand about how fractional pricing
   works? What do they think they're buying versus what they're buying?

6. Hourly versus retainer versus project: which do you use, which do you refuse,
   and what happened the last time you tried the one you now refuse?

7. What's the real cost comparison against a full-time CMO once you include
   recruiting, equity, benefits, and ramp? Use numbers you've actually seen.

8. What hidden costs bite clients that nobody puts on a pricing page?

9. Have you ever raised your rate mid-engagement or fired a client over price?
   What happened?

10. If a founder has $5K a month, $15K a month, or $30K a month, what should
    each of them actually buy? Be blunt if one of those answers is "not this."
```

## Prompt 3: `/when-to-hire-a-fractional-cmo` (new page)

*Targets a cluster with absurd ratios: `why hire a fractional cmo` (difficulty 2, traffic potential 6,100), `who should hire a fractional cmo` (difficulty 1, TP 3,900), `who needs a fractional cmo` (TP 3,500), `when to hire` (150, difficulty 2), `should i hire`, `is a fractional cmo worth it`. One strong page can take the whole cluster. The "do not hire one if" section is the part that will actually rank.*

```
I'm writing a decision page: should you hire a fractional CMO, and when.
I want the honest version including who should NOT hire one. Interview me.
Here's my dump:

1. What has to be true about a company before a fractional CMO is the right
   move? Revenue, stage, team, what else?

2. Describe the specific company profile where this works best. Then describe
   the profile where it reliably fails.

3. What are the three or four signals a founder can check themselves that mean
   "you need this now"?

4. What are the signals that mean "you need something else"? An agency, a
   full-time hire, a contractor, or nothing yet?

5. Tell me about a company you turned down or that ended badly, where the
   honest read was that they never should have hired a fractional at all.

6. What do founders usually get wrong about the timing? Too early or too late,
   and what does the mistake cost them?

7. If a company already has a marketing manager or a head of growth, does a
   fractional CMO still make sense? What does that look like when it works?

8. How should someone tell a good fractional CMO from a repackaged freelancer
   during the interview? Give me the specific questions and the answers that
   should scare them off.

9. What's the honest expected timeline to something measurable? Not the
   sales-pitch answer.

10. When has hiring you NOT been worth it, and would you say so publicly?
```

## Prompt 4: `/ai-native-fractional-cmo` (the wedge)

*Targets `ai cmo` (300, difficulty 3, CPC $450), plus the whole AI-native positioning. This is your moat: everyone claims AI, you actually shipped the systems. This page needs receipts, not claims.*

```
I'm writing the page that explains what "AI-native fractional CMO" actually
means when I say it. Everyone in my category claims AI. I've actually built the
systems. I want this page to be proof, not positioning. Interview me. Here's my
dump:

1. Go system by system through what you've actually built: Billy CRM, AdIntel,
   the email validation utility, Fitagentic. For each one: what problem, what
   it replaced, roughly what that thing used to cost, how long it took you to
   build, and whether it's still running.

2. Which of those did NOT work, or you'd build differently now? What broke?

3. What's the honest hours-and-dollars picture? Where has AI actually saved
   real money in your own operation versus where has it been a wash?

4. Walk me through one thing you do for clients today that would have been
   impossible or uneconomic for you two years ago. Be concrete.

5. What can you charge less for now because of this, and what still costs the
   same because the bottleneck was never the labor?

6. When a competitor says they're "AI-powered," what should a buyer ask them to
   prove it? What question would expose someone who isn't?

7. What are you deliberately NOT automating in client work, and why?

8. What's the most overhyped AI claim in marketing services right now? Name it.

9. Where has AI made your work measurably worse or created a problem you had to
   undo?

10. What does a client actually get from this that they wouldn't get from a
    traditional fractional CMO? Answer as if they're skeptical, because they
    are.
```

## Reusable template for future pieces

When we add a fifth, sixth, tenth piece, the prompt gets built the same way:

1. **Name the target cluster and the ratio.** Volume, difficulty, traffic potential, and what the SERP is currently rewarding. This sets the angle before a word is written.
2. **State the angle in one sentence**, and make it a position rather than a topic.
3. **Write 8 to 12 questions, each of which fails if answered generically.** Good shapes:
   - "Walk me through the last three times X happened."
   - "Name a time you were wrong about X."
   - "What do you refuse to do, and why?"
   - "What does everyone else in this category get wrong?"
   - "What did that specific client actually say?"
   Bad shapes: "What are the benefits of X?", "What do you think about X?", "Why is X important?"
4. **Always include at least one question that costs Bill something to answer.** A failure, a refusal, a client that ended badly. That is the material nobody can copy and it is the exact signal Google is currently rewarding on this SERP.
5. **Always include one question that produces a number** he can stand behind.
