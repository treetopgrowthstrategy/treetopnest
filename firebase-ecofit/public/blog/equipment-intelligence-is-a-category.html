Equipment Intelligence Is a Category Now. Guesswork Is the Competition.

Dave Johnson, Ecofit  ·  August 5, 2026

For most of the history of this industry, the most expensive decisions on the floor were made on a feeling.

Which cardio to buy. How many squat racks. Whether to add another row of treadmills or pull two and put in turf. Ask ten operators how they made those calls and you will hear ten versions of the same answer. Experience. Gut. What the rep recommended. What the club down the street just did. What sold well last cycle.

None of that is data. All of it is a guess wearing a nicer outfit.

I am not saying operators are careless. The opposite. The people who run clubs are some of the sharpest operators in any industry, because they have had to be. When the only instrument you have is your own judgment, you sharpen your judgment. But judgment has a ceiling, and that ceiling is the floor itself. You cannot watch every machine, every hour, across every location. You see the morning rush and the evening crush and you extrapolate. The rest is invisible.

That invisible part is where the money is.

Here is the shift that is happening right now, whether you have a name for it or not. The fitness floor is becoming measurable. Every piece of equipment, connected or not, can tell you how often it is used, by whom, for how long, and whether it is about to fail. Not in a quarterly report that lands stale. In real time. The same way your watch reads your heart rate, the floor can read itself.

That capability needs a name, so here is the name. Equipment intelligence.

And once a category has a name, it has a before and an after. Before, you bought on instinct and hoped. After, you buy on usage and know. Before, a layout was an argument between the architect, the rep, and whoever in the room was loudest. After, the floor settles the argument, because the floor keeps the receipts.

The operators who are early to this are not smarter than the ones who are not. They just stopped guessing first.

Think about what that actually changes. You are about to spend six figures refreshing a floor. The old way, you replace what looks tired and you trust the catalog. The new way, you study six weeks of usage and discover that half of what looked tired is your most-used equipment and should be protected, and the shiny thing the rep is pushing would sit dead in a corner. Same budget. Completely different order. One of those operators grows. The other one finds out in eighteen months.

Here is what that looks like in the real numbers. Take the same ten categories and rank them a second way, not by how much total usage they carry, but by how many hours each individual machine runs per day. The order falls apart.

Same equipment. Different order.

Share of usage tells you what your members did. Use per unit tells you how hard each machine had to work to deliver it. When a category ranks far higher on workload than on share, the floor is telling you there are not enough of them.

This is why I keep saying guesswork is the competition. Not the club across town. Not the chain with the bigger marketing budget. The thing quietly costing operators the most is the confident decision that turns out to be wrong, and the reason it stays wrong is that nobody measured it.

Equipment intelligence is how you find out which of your certainties are real.

None of this requires ripping out your floor and starting over. That is the part operators get wrong. You do not need a building full of brand new connected consoles to get smart data. The technology to measure the equipment you already own exists today, and we put it on the floor you have. Old iron and new touchscreens, free weights and cardio, the turf and the racks. If members touch it, it can be measured.

So the operators waiting on a full refresh before they get serious about data have it backwards. The data is what tells you whether you need the refresh at all, and when you do refresh, the data is where the return comes from. Nobody should be signing a five to seven year lease on a guess.

And it is not only your floor talking. We have close to 10,000 clubs on the network, so when we make a recommendation we are reading your usage against what the rest of the industry is doing. Your data tells you what happened. The network tells you where it is going. If your stepmills and your ellipticals are both running hot, that is useful. Knowing that stepmill demand is climbing faster than elliptical demand across the market is what actually changes the order. That is the difference between a report and a recommendation.

Once the floor is measurable, it becomes predictable. The same data that tells you what gets used tells you what is about to break, because a machine signals its own decline long before a member feels it. You stop waiting two weeks for a report and start asking your floor a question and getting the answer in the room. That is the direction the whole category is moving, and it is moving fast.

Equipment intelligence is here. Guesswork is the competition. The operators who understand that are already pulling away, quietly, while everyone else is still arguing in a conference room about how many stair climbers to put on the floor.

For the record, we know the answer to that one. Everything above comes out of our 2025 North American Annual Ranking Report, built on a network of close to 10,000 clubs.
