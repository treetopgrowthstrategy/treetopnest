# Rescue prompts: the three thin pages worth saving

Three pages survived the September prune because Bill has genuine first-hand
experience behind them. The other eight in the same cluster were deleted, because
no honest version of "Claude for musicians" exists when nobody here has worked
with a musician.

These three are currently template output: roughly 500 words, no operator voice,
no named client, no original data. That shape is what the Helpful Content
classifier punished this domain for in June 2026. Adding length alone makes it
worse, not better. What changes the page is material only Bill can supply.

**How to use these:** Bill answers into a voice memo or a raw dump. Claude shapes
the answer into the page. Claude does not invent the answer. If a question cannot
be answered from real experience, the page does not get that section, and if most
questions cannot be answered, the page should be deleted rather than padded.

Hard constraints for the rewrite:
- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere in the output.
- No invented clients, numbers, quotes, or anecdotes. Ever.
- Every claim traceable to something Bill actually did or measured.
- Name real companies only where Bill confirms consent, matching how the existing
  case studies disclose it.
- If a section would be generic advice available on any other site, cut it.

## 1. /fractional-vp-sales

The strongest of the three. Bill has been a fractional revenue executive for a
decade, so this page can be written entirely from the inside. The current version
reads like a definition page written by someone who has never held the seat.

1. You have been the fractional revenue exec. What does a company actually hire a
   fractional VP Sales to fix, in the words they use when they call you? Not the
   polished version, the thing they actually say on the first call.
2. What is the difference in practice between hiring a fractional VP Sales and a
   fractional CRO? Where does the VP Sales title get used when CRO is what they
   need, or the reverse?
3. Describe an engagement where the fractional VP Sales model was the wrong answer
   and you said so. What did you recommend instead, and what did it cost you to
   say it?
4. What does the first 30 days look like concretely? Name the artifacts you
   produce, the meetings you take over, the numbers you ask for on day one.
5. What do founders consistently get wrong about what this person can do in a
   quarter?
6. What is the honest pricing range, and what changes it? Tie this to the
   fractional executive pricing report already on the site.
7. When a fractional VP Sales engagement fails, what is the usual cause? Be
   specific about the failure mode, not "poor communication".
8. What should a buyer ask in the interview that would expose someone who has not
   actually carried a number?

## 2. /how-to-write-okrs-with-claude

Bill runs GTM planning for real companies. The page currently explains OKRs
generically, which every site does. The defensible version is his actual planning
workflow with Claude in it.

1. Walk through the last real quarterly planning cycle you ran. What did you
   actually put into Claude, and at which step?
2. What does Claude do badly in OKR work that people assume it does well? Where
   have you had to throw its output away?
3. What goes in the Project knowledge base before the OKR conversation is worth
   having? Name the documents.
4. Show a real before and after: a weak objective as it first came out, and what
   it became after the pass. Scrub the client name if needed, keep the substance.
5. How do you keep OKRs from becoming a list of activities instead of outcomes,
   and where does Claude help or hurt that?
6. What is your actual check-in cadence, and what does Claude do at each check-in?
7. What would you tell a team that wants to generate OKRs from scratch with AI
   and skip the argument? What do they lose?

## 3. /claude-for-restaurants

The weakest claim of the three and the one most likely to be deleted instead.
Bill does not operate restaurants. He sells green coffee into restaurants, cafes
and hotels through El Quetzal and Kapej, which is buyer-side knowledge of how
these businesses actually run and buy.

Answer question 1 first. If the answer is no, delete the page.

1. Do you have enough real contact with restaurant and cafe operators to say
   something non-obvious about their operations? Yes or no. A no here means the
   page comes down, and that is the right call.
2. From the wholesale coffee side, what do you actually know about how these
   operators run their week? Where does their time go?
3. What have you seen operators get wrong about AI, from real conversations, not
   from reading about the industry?
4. Where is the genuine leverage for an independent operator: menu, ordering,
   staffing, supplier comms, reviews? Rank them from what you have observed.
5. What has El Quetzal or Kapej itself automated in dealing with these buyers, and
   what did it change? This is first-party and defensible.
6. What would you refuse to recommend to a restaurant, and why?
