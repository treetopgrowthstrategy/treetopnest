Email Strategy: From Retention Channel to Acquisition Engine

The Shift That Changes Everything

With Klaviyo now tying individual transactions to specific emails, Forkful has crossed a threshold most brands never reach: true email-level attribution. This isn't just a reporting upgrade — it's a fundamentally different way to run the email program. Every send is now a controlled experiment. Every meal feature is now testable. Every subject line is now measurable against revenue, not just opens.

Priority 3: Meal-Level Acquisition Testing Methodology

The core question we now have the infrastructure to answer: Which specific meals drive new customer acquisition vs. re-engaging existing subscribers? This is a different question than "which emails perform best" — and the answer should determine how we build the acquisition content calendar, which meals get ad spend, and which meals get featured in the top of the welcome flow.

Step 1: Segment the List

In Klaviyo, create two clean cohorts: New subscribers (0 purchases) and Active customers (1+ purchases). Every test runs on both cohorts simultaneously. The same meal featured to both groups will produce different CVR — that delta is the acquisition signal.

Step 2: Tag the SKU

Every email campaign featuring a specific meal gets a Klaviyo event tag linking that send to the featured SKU. When a purchase occurs within the attribution window, Klaviyo records which meal was featured. Over 8–12 sends, a pattern emerges: certain meals consistently convert new subscribers at a higher rate.

Step 3: Define the Window

Set a consistent attribution window for all acquisition tests: 72 hours post-send for new subscriber conversion. 7-day window for re-engagement. Consistency matters more than the specific window — pick one and don't change it mid-test or the data becomes incomparable.

Priority 4: Email as an Acquisition Channel

Most prepared meal brands treat email as a retention tool. The list gets campaigns, the campaigns drive repeat orders, repeat orders are measured as email revenue. That model undersells the channel. With proper list growth infrastructure, email can function as a cost-effective acquisition engine — particularly for a brand with a 93% paid traffic dependency and an $84 paid CAC.

List Growth Infrastructure Needed
  • 01Content-gated lead magnets — GLP-1 meal plan PDF, seed-oil-free shopping guide, macro calculator. Capture email before the purchase decision.
  • 02Popup with meal-specific offer — not a generic 10% off, but "Get our top-rated GLP-1 meal free on your first order." Specificity converts.
  • 03Podcast coupon codes → email capture — every podcast sponsor code should land on a page that collects email before revealing the discount. Converts podcast audience into owned list.
  • 04Referral-triggered email flows — when a referred customer signs up, trigger a sequence that's optimized for referred buyer behavior (higher intent, warmer, different objections).
Welcome Flow Architecture (New Subscribers)
  • Day 0Hero email: top acquisition meal (data-driven from sprint above), lead with clean ingredients story, one clear CTA.
  • Day 2Objection handling: price comparison vs. Factor. "You're already spending $11.49/meal there — here's what you're not getting."
  • Day 4Social proof: UGC-style photography, real customer language, seed-oil-free / GLP-1 angle if segmentation data suggests health intent.
  • Day 7Urgency + offer expiry. The discount offered at signup expires today. This email should be the highest-revenue send in the flow.
  • Day 14Re-engagement for non-converters: different angle, different meal, different offer structure. If no open — trigger SMS fallback.
The North Star Metric for Email

Revenue per subscriber per month (RPS/mo). Not open rate. Not click rate. Not even revenue per send. RPS/mo captures the full value of the list — acquisition efficiency, retention performance, and send frequency in a single number. Once you have 90 days of Klaviyo attribution data, set a baseline RPS/mo and make every email decision against it. This is the metric that tells you whether the list is a growth asset or a maintenance cost.
